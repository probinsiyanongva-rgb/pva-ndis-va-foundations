2. Why This Matters

Beginners often collect long lists of terms without learning what the terms mean in actual work.

Your glossary should answer two questions:

  1. What does this term mean?
  2. What does this mean for my VA role?

The second question protects your boundaries.

3. Learn

Start with these terms:

Term Plain-English starting point
NDISAustralia's national disability scheme
NDIAAustralian Government agency that administers the NDIS
ParticipantPerson who has applied and meets NDIS eligibility criteria
ProviderPerson or organization delivering supports or services
NDIS CommissionIndependent Commonwealth regulator focused on quality and safety of NDIS supports and services
Support coordinatorA role that can help a participant coordinate supports, where included in their plan
Plan managerA role/provider that handles plan-management functions for a participant
Support workerWorker who delivers support/services within their role and provider arrangements

These are orientation definitions, not substitutes for the official source.

Source/review note

For each term, record:

  • official source;
  • date checked;
  • what you verified;
  • whether the term is likely to change or depend on the participant's circumstances.

The NDIS Glossary is a useful starting point for current terminology.

4. See It

Example glossary entry:

Participant

Plain English: A person who has applied for and meets the eligibility criteria for the NDIS.

VA relevance: A VA may handle administrative information connected with a participant, but should follow provider procedures for access, disclosure, records and decisions.

Source: NDIS Glossary

Checked: 25 September 2026

Boundary note: The VA does not decide who is eligible.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question

Why should the glossary include a source and review date?

Model answer

Because NDIS terminology, systems and rules can change. A source and review date show where the definition came from and when it was checked.

Question

What is the difference between knowing what “participant” means and deciding whether someone is eligible?

Model answer

Knowing the term is administrative knowledge. Deciding eligibility is an authorized NDIS decision and is outside the beginner VA role.

Micro-proof

Take one glossary entry and add this line:

Boundary: I can explain the administrative meaning; I do not make the related eligibility, funding or clinical decision.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

A glossary is a learning and reference tool.

It does not give the learner authority to:

  • approve funding;
  • determine eligibility;
  • provide clinical advice;
  • interpret a participant's plan as a professional decision-maker;
  • make regulatory determinations.

When a real task depends on current rules or a participant-specific decision, return to the appropriate current source and provider workflow.

Lesson 5.2 — What a VA Can Explain

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A good administrative answer starts with evidence.

Before you reply, ask:

Is this based on a current official source, a provider record, a documented provider procedure, or my assumption?

← Module 5 overview

2. Why This Matters

A VA may have access to information without having authority to make the decision connected to that information.

For example, you may be able to:

  • locate a provider procedure;
  • explain where a document is stored;
  • summarize a current official page;
  • identify missing information;
  • record a question;
  • prepare a message for review;
  • route a funding question.

You may not be the person authorized to decide the participant's funding, eligibility or clinical suitability.

3. Learn

Use three levels when deciding what you can explain.

Level 1 — Documented fact

You can communicate a fact that is clearly supported by the relevant record or current official source.

Example:

“The current NDIS page says the final eligibility decision is made by the NDIA.”

Level 2 — Provider procedure

You can explain a documented Wattlebird process when you are working inside the fictional training environment.

Example:

“Wattlebird's fictional procedure says invoice questions are routed to the responsible finance/operations contact.”

Level 3 — Decision or professional judgment

When the question asks you to decide something outside your role, route it.

Examples:

  • “Will NDIS fund this?”
  • “Is this support clinically suitable?”
  • “Is this participant eligible?”
  • “Can we approve this exception?”
  • “Can this worker continue this shift?”

A useful response can be:

Acknowledge → identify what you can check → refer → record/follow up

This keeps the conversation moving without pretending to have authority you do not have.

4. See It

Open:

Inbox → E03

E03 is from Anna Mitchell, a parent of a referral:

Her daughter Grace was recently approved for the NDIS. She asks whether Wattlebird has space for community access support and what the NDIS covers.

There are two different questions here:

  1. Does Wattlebird have capacity?
  2. What does the NDIS cover?

The first may involve provider availability.

The second is an NDIS funding question and should not be answered from memory.

A VA can acknowledge the question, identify what information is available, and route the funding question to the appropriate person.

5. Try It

Draft a short response to E03.

Your response should:

  • acknowledge the enquiry;
  • separate provider availability from the funding question;
  • avoid promising that the NDIS will fund a particular support;
  • state what you can check or route;
  • give a next step.

Next-Action Test

  • What do I open? Inbox

  • What happens after? Follow up once the responsible person or source provides the needed information

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question

Which basis is safest for an NDIS-related statement?

A. Something you remember from a previous job.

B. A current official source or documented provider information that applies to the question.

C. What another VA told you last year.

Model answer

B.

Current or provider-specific information should be checked against the source that actually governs the task.

Micro-proof

Rewrite this:

“Yes, NDIS will fund that.”

A safer administrative version is:

“I can check the current information available to us and route the funding question to the appropriate person for confirmation.”

8. Work Boundary

The VA can:

  • locate information;
  • summarize documented information;
  • identify what is missing;
  • ask clarifying questions;
  • prepare a response;
  • route the question;
  • track the follow-up.

The VA should not independently determine:

  • eligibility;
  • funding approval;
  • clinical suitability;
  • legal entitlement;
  • regulatory status.

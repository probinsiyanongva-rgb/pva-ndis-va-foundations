Lesson 5.3 — The Safe Response to “Does NDIS Fund This?”

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

You are not being tested on how confidently you can answer a difficult question.

You are being tested on whether you can recognize the question, stay within role, and continue the workflow safely.

← Module 5 overview

2. Why This Matters

The wrong answer can sound helpful:

“Yes, that should be covered.”

But a funding question may depend on current rules, the participant's plan, the support involved, and the circumstances.

Clinical suitability is a different question again.

Eligibility is another decision area.

The VA's strength is the administrative process around the question.

3. Learn

Use this response pattern:

1. Acknowledge

Show that you understood the question.

“Thanks for checking.”

2. Avoid unsupported advice

Do not make the decision yourself.

“I don't want to give you an incorrect answer from memory.”

3. Refer

Identify the appropriate person or current official source.

“I can route the question to the appropriate person for confirmation.”

4. Record and follow up

When the task requires follow-up:

“I'll record the question and follow up once we have confirmation.”

A complete training example

“I understand why you're checking. I can review the information available to us and route the question to the appropriate person. I'll note the question and follow up once we have confirmation.”

This is an administrative response pattern, not a script that must be copied word-for-word.

5. Try It

For each prompt, write a response containing:

  1. acknowledgment;
  2. safe boundary;
  3. appropriate referral or source-check step;
  4. follow-up statement where appropriate.

Do not answer the underlying funding, clinical or eligibility question yourself.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

SQ-01 — Funding

Model response:

“Thanks for checking. I can review the current NDIS information and the relevant provider/participant records, then route the question for confirmation. I'll record the question and follow up once we have an answer.”

SQ-02 — Clinical suitability

Model response:

“Thanks for asking. Clinical suitability is something the appropriate qualified professional should determine. I can record the question and route it to the relevant person for review.”

SQ-03 — Eligibility

Model response:

“Thanks for checking. Eligibility is determined through the NDIS process, so I shouldn't make that determination myself. I can record the question and direct it to the appropriate NDIS contact or provider staff.”

What the three responses have in common

They:

  • acknowledge;
  • identify the boundary;
  • route the decision;
  • keep the administrative workflow moving.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

A VA can support the process around a decision.

That can include:

  • recording the question;
  • finding the correct source;
  • gathering the information the authorized person needs;
  • preparing a draft;
  • sending the question through the approved channel;
  • tracking the response;
  • closing the loop.

The VA does not become the decision-maker simply because the question arrived in the inbox.

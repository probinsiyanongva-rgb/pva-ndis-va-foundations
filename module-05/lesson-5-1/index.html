2. Why This Matters

A participant may ask:

“Does NDIS fund this?”

A family member may ask:

“Is she eligible?”

A worker may ask:

“Who decides whether this support can go ahead?”

These questions can sound simple while involving different parts of the NDIS system.

A VA becomes more useful when they can recognize the subject of the question and route it appropriately.

3. Learn

The NDIS

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is Australia's national scheme for people with disability. The NDIS provides funding to eligible people for NDIS supports and also connects people with community and mainstream supports.

The NDIA

The National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) is the Australian Government organization that administers the NDIS.

The current NDIS glossary describes the NDIA as administering the NDIS. Current NDIS guidance also explains that the NDIA is responsible for decisions such as eligibility and plan funding decisions.

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (NDIS Commission) is an independent Commonwealth regulator focused on the quality and safety of NDIS supports and services.

The Commission regulates and registers NDIS providers, monitors compliance with the NDIS Code of Conduct and Practice Standards where applicable, and responds to concerns, complaints and reportable incidents within its role.

Participants

A participant is a person who has applied for and meets the eligibility criteria for the NDIS.

A participant has an individual plan describing their NDIS supports and funding.

Providers

A provider delivers supports or services to NDIS participants.

Provider registration requirements depend on the services and circumstances involved. The VA's job is to work from the provider's actual procedures and current official information when registration or regulatory status matters.

A useful distinction

Think of the organizations this way:

Question area Relevant organization / role
NDIS access and eligibilityNDIA / appropriate NDIS contact
Participant plan and funding decisionsNDIA / authorized NDIS decision-maker
Provider quality and safety regulationNDIS Commission
Provider's internal operationsProvider management
Participant-specific support coordinationSupport coordinator, where applicable
Participant-specific plan-management workflowPlan manager, where applicable
Routine administrationVA, within role

This table is an orientation tool. It is not a complete map of every NDIS function.

4. See It

M5_Situations

You will see five fictional situations:

  • SIT-01 — Participant asks what NDIS funding covers.
  • SIT-02 — Invoice batch needs an administrative pre-check.
  • SIT-03 — Participant asks for copies of records.
  • SIT-04 — Worker reports a participant injury.
  • SIT-05 — Agreement draft needs approval.

Notice that the situations do not all belong to the same person.

For example:

  • an administrative pre-check can be part of the VA's work;
  • a participant funding question may need an appropriate NDIS/provider contact;
  • an injury requires the provider's safety and incident process;
  • an agreement draft may be prepared by the VA but approved by the authorized person.

8. Work Boundary

A VA can learn enough NDIS context to recognize the subject of a request and route it correctly.

A VA should not turn this orientation into independent decisions about:

  • NDIS eligibility;
  • whether a participant will receive funding;
  • whether a particular support will be funded;
  • clinical suitability;
  • regulatory compliance;
  • provider-level approval.

When a decision belongs to an authorized person or organization, the VA documents and routes it.

Lesson 6.2 — Current Support-Budget Structure

1. Progress Reminder

You have learned to extract information from a plan without turning administrative reading into professional decision-making.

Now we look at how support budgets, categories and support items are structured.

← Module 6 overview

2. Why This Matters

NDIS information can change.

That means an NDIS VA needs two skills:

  1. understanding the structure well enough to navigate administrative work;
  2. knowing when to check the current official source.

As of the current-source review for this course, the NDIS describes four support budgets:

  • Core supports
  • Capacity Building supports
  • Capital supports
  • Recurring supports

The NDIS also states that not every participant has funding in all four budgets.

The NDIS explains that support categories sit within support budgets, and support items sit within categories.

3. Learn

Think of the structure as:

PLAN → SUPPORT BUDGET → SUPPORT CATEGORY → SUPPORT ITEM

This is a learning model for navigating information. It is not a substitute for reading the participant's actual plan or current official source.

Support budgets

The current NDIS guidance identifies four support budgets. A participant may have funding in only the budgets relevant to their plan.

Support categories

Categories organize supports within a budget. The NDIS currently describes 21 support categories.

Support items

Support items are the specific kinds of NDIS supports that can be used. The NDIS explains that support items have codes and price limits, with current pricing information maintained separately.

Important current-information note

The NDIS currently warns that some website information is being updated to reflect new legislation. Treat current structures as information to verify, not facts to memorize permanently.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What is the transferable skill in this lesson?

Model answer:
Being able to locate and interpret the current official structure, then verify the participant's actual information. The goal is not memorizing a permanent number of budgets or categories.

Question: If a real provider's plan does not look exactly like the Wattlebird training plan, what should you do?

Model answer:
Follow the provider's authorised system/workflow, check the current official information where relevant, and ask the appropriate person if the structure or meaning is unclear.

8. Work Boundary

The VA can identify and summarize documented information.

The VA should not infer that a support is funded simply because it sounds related to a budget or category.

For real work, the participant's actual plan, current official guidance and provider workflow control.

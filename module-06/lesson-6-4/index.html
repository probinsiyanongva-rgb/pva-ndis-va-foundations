2. Why This Matters

A real administrative task rarely arrives as a perfectly organized question.

You may receive:

“Can we proceed with this support request for Liam?”

Your first job is to determine what is actually known.

3. Learn

Use the Module 6 decision sequence:

DO → CHECK → CLASSIFY → ASK / HAND OFF → FOLLOW UP → CLOSE THE LOOP

DO

Open the correct Wattlebird training records.

CHECK

Compare the request with the information actually shown.

CLASSIFY

Use:

  • OK — the administrative evidence is sufficient to proceed within role.
  • FLAG — something needs attention or verification.
  • ASK — a clarification is required before proceeding.
  • ESCALATE — an authorised person needs to decide or urgent responsibility applies.

ASK / HAND OFF

Send a focused question to the appropriate person.

FOLLOW UP

Record who owns the next action and when you should check back.

CLOSE THE LOOP

Record the outcome when the unresolved point is completed.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

SR-01 — Weekly community access support

The fictional plan shows a Core allocation, and the fictional category breakdown includes community participation.

A learner may identify this as the closest fictional administrative match.

The learner still does not independently decide whether the support is authorised in real NDIS work.

SR-02 — Physiotherapy appointment

The fictional plan contains a Capacity Building allocation and an allied-health-related fictional administrative example.

The learner can identify the relevant context.

The learner should not decide clinical suitability or funding approval.

SR-03 — New support request with unclear category

The request itself says the category is unclear.

The correct administrative response is to identify the missing information and ask a focused clarification question.

Example:

“Could you confirm which plan category or approved support this request should be recorded against before I update the participant record?”

8. Work Boundary

The Module 6 VA role is administrative.

You can:

  • locate information;
  • summarize documented information;
  • organize records;
  • identify missing information;
  • ask focused questions;
  • route work;
  • document follow-up.

You do not independently:

  • determine eligibility;
  • determine clinical suitability;
  • approve funding;
  • decide that an unclear support is authorised;
  • override the participant's plan;
  • change management arrangements.

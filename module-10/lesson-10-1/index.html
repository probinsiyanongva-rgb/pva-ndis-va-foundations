Lesson 10.1 — From Referral to Onboarding

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A new referral starts a chain of administrative tasks. Your job is to make that chain visible and organized.

← Module 10 overview

2. Why This Matters

A referral can look complete while important onboarding items remain unresolved.

A reliable VA can answer:

  • What has arrived?
  • What is still missing?
  • Who owns the missing item?
  • What happens next?
  • When should we follow up?

That information makes the handover easier and reduces avoidable delays.

ONB-09Welcome / communication
ONB-10Handover / closure

Notice the columns:

Evidence / Field → Owner → Status → Follow-Up Trigger → Closure Condition

This is more useful than a simple list of tick boxes because an incomplete item needs a path to resolution.

The key distinction

Missing means the required information or evidence is not present.

Waiting on someone means you have identified the missing item and another person or process is expected to provide or confirm it.

Record that dependency rather than treating the item as complete.

4. See It

In the Wattlebird scenario, Grace Mitchell is a new referral.

The referral should lead you into the onboarding checklist. You are not being asked to decide whether Grace is eligible for the NDIS or whether a particular support is clinically appropriate.

Your administrative job is to identify what the provider needs to complete its process and route decision questions to the appropriate owner.

Example:

Finding: Management type / plan context is not confirmed.

Classification: FLAG

Next action: Record the gap and route it to the appropriate Wattlebird owner.

Boundary: Do not guess the management type.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What is the difference between “missing” and “waiting on someone”?

Model answer:

“Missing” describes the current state: the required information or evidence is not available.

“Waiting on someone” describes the next state of the workflow: the missing item has an identified owner or dependency and needs follow-up.

A strong record makes both visible.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

You may track onboarding information and identify gaps.

You do not independently decide:

  • NDIS eligibility;
  • funding;
  • clinical suitability;
  • participant consent/authority;
  • provider approval;
  • service terms.

When a decision is required, route the question to the authorized owner.

Lesson 10.3 — Service Agreement as an Administrative Draft

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A service agreement contains provider-participant terms. Your role in this exercise is administrative preparation and tracking.

← Module 10 overview

2. Why This Matters

The NDIS describes a service agreement as a signed agreement between a participant and provider. It can describe supports, delivery arrangements, costs, responsibilities, changes, review arrangements and what happens when concerns arise.

That means a VA needs a clear boundary between:

preparing a draft

and

agreeing to the terms.

3. Learn

Open:

M10_Agreement

The Wattlebird template is a fictional training template and is explicitly:

DRAFT — NOT A LEGAL AGREEMENT

Your task is to populate administrative information that is already supported by the training records.

Examples of information you may prepare include:

  • participant name;
  • provider name;
  • known service details;
  • known dates;
  • documented schedule information;
  • administrative notes;
  • fields clearly identified as requiring manager review.

If a term is unclear, leave it for review or ask the appropriate owner.

Do not invent a price, cancellation term, service commitment, or other provider term.

The NDIS says providers may use a standard service-agreement template, and participants should be supported to understand what the agreement means before signing.

4. See It

A good draft might contain:

Status: DRAFT — NOT A LEGAL AGREEMENT
Participant: Grace Mitchell
Provider: Wattlebird Support Services
Known service information: entered from the fictional onboarding record
Unconfirmed terms: marked for manager review

Notice the difference between recording a known fact and creating a term.

If a fictional price or agreement term has not been supplied by an authorized Wattlebird source, mark it for review.

5. Try It

Open:

M10_Agreement

Complete the administrative fields that can be supported by the fictional records.

For every field you cannot safely complete:

  1. leave it unresolved;
  2. identify what needs clarification;
  3. identify the owner;
  4. record the follow-up.

Keep the document clearly marked as a draft.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Which fields can a VA prepare, and which require authorized review?

Model answer:

A VA can prepare administrative fields supported by existing records and clearly documented provider information. Terms that require negotiation, approval, interpretation, pricing decisions, or other authorized judgment should be marked for review and routed to the appropriate owner.

8. Work Boundary

You may prepare and track a draft.

You do not independently:

  • negotiate provider terms;
  • approve the agreement;
  • promise a service arrangement;
  • create unsupported pricing or cancellation terms;
  • decide clinical or funding matters.

The NDIS describes a service agreement as an agreement between the participant and provider; your training draft is evidence of administrative preparation, not evidence that you have authority to agree to the provider's terms.

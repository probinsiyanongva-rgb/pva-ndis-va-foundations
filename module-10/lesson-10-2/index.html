Lesson 10.2 — Consent, Authority and Missing Information

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

Good onboarding records separate facts from assumptions.

That becomes especially important when someone other than the participant communicates with the provider.

← Module 10 overview

2. Why This Matters

Information can be accurate and still be unsafe to act on if the provider has not confirmed who is authorized to provide or receive it.

The VA should document what is known and route authority questions through the provider's approved process.

3. Learn

Open E03 in the Wattlebird Inbox.

The referral comes from Anna Mitchell regarding Grace Mitchell.

For this exercise, do not assume from the relationship alone that Anna has authority to make decisions, receive information, or agree to provider terms.

The NDIS explains that consent controls who can access information or act on a person's behalf. The NDIS also distinguishes consent from the checks needed to confirm that the provider is speaking with the right person.

For the course, translate that into an administrative habit:

Identify → Record → Verify through the approved process → Route if unclear

Do not create extra data-collection tasks simply because more information might be useful someday.

Ask for what is required for the current workflow.

4. See It

Suppose the onboarding record says:

“Parent sent referral. Consent/authority not confirmed.”

A weak administrative response would be to treat “parent” as proof of authority.

A stronger response is:

Finding: Consent/authority is not confirmed in the onboarding record.

Classification: FLAG

Next action: Record the missing authority evidence and route it to the authorized Wattlebird process.

Follow-up: Track the owner and the agreed trigger.

Boundary: Do not independently decide whether Anna can authorize access or make decisions for Grace.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should you do when the referral information is incomplete but you are unsure whether the person providing it has authority?

Model answer:

Record the known facts, identify the missing authority evidence, and route the question through the provider's approved consent/authority process. Do not assume authority from the person's relationship to the participant.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Create at least one clear follow-up entry for an unresolved onboarding item.

Record it in the appropriate Wattlebird tracking process and include:

  • owner;
  • next action;
  • channel;
  • follow-up trigger;
  • closure condition.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

You may identify an authority gap and document it.

You do not independently decide that a family member, nominee, representative or other person has authority.

The provider's authorized process determines what evidence is required.

9. Next Step

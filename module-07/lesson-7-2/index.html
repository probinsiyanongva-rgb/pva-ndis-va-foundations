2. Why This Matters

Two requests may look similar but require different handling.

For this course, use four classifications:

Classification Meaning
OKEvidence is sufficient and you can proceed/complete within role.
FLAGSomething needs attention or verification. Record it and route it.
ASKClarification is required before proceeding.
ESCALATEA person with authority or urgent responsibility needs to act or decide.

Important distinction

A FLAG is not automatically closed.

If something is flagged and remains unresolved, record:

  • what was found;
  • why it matters;
  • owner/recipient;
  • next action;
  • channel;
  • follow-up trigger;
  • closure condition.

3. Learn

Use this sequence:

What do I know? → What is missing? → Who owns the decision? → What happens next?

OK

Use OK when the evidence is sufficient and the action is within your role.

FLAG

Use FLAG when something needs attention or verification but the situation does not itself require an authority or urgent responsible person to take over immediately.

ASK

Use ASK when a missing fact or instruction prevents you from proceeding safely or accurately.

ESCALATE

Use ESCALATE when:

  • an authorised person needs to decide;
  • the matter involves urgent responsibility;
  • the matter is outside the VA's authority and needs an active handoff.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

Example 1

A routine document has the correct participant ID, date, and required fields.

Classification: OK

You can complete the administrative action within the workflow.

Example 2

A record contains a mismatch that needs verification.

Classification: FLAG

Record what you found, route it, and track the follow-up.

Example 3

A request says “Please process this” but does not identify which participant or record it belongs to.

Classification: ASK

Request the missing information.

Example 4

A worker reports an injury involving a participant during support.

Classification: ESCALATE

The matter requires an authorised responsible person to act under the provider's incident/safeguarding procedure.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What makes FLAG different from ESCALATE?

Model answer: FLAG identifies something requiring attention or verification. ESCALATE means an authorised person or urgent responsible person needs to act or decide.

Question: Does every FLAG mean the task is finished?

Model answer: No. An unresolved FLAG needs an owner, next action, follow-up trigger, and closure condition.

8. Work Boundary

Classification does not create authority.

For example, identifying a funding question as ESCALATE does not mean the VA gets to answer the funding question.

The classification tells you how to route the work.

2. Why This Matters

NDIS provider work is collaborative.

A request may involve:

  • provider operations/admin;
  • a support coordinator;
  • a plan manager;
  • a support worker;
  • an allied-health professional;
  • management or another authorised person.

A VA's job is to recognize likely ownership, route the work, and keep the handoff traceable.

3. Learn

Build your role map around the work being requested, not just job titles.

Provider operations/admin

Typical administrative ownership can include:

  • scheduling and roster administration;
  • document tracking;
  • routine operational coordination;
  • internal follow-up;
  • administrative records.

Support Coordinator

May be the appropriate role for participant-support coordination questions and matters involving coordination of supports.

Plan Manager

Handles plan-management-related financial administration within their authorised workflow.

Support Worker

Provides direct support. A VA may coordinate or document administrative information around a support worker's work, but does not direct clinical or participant-care decisions outside authorised procedure.

Allied Health

Handles professional/clinical matters within their discipline.

Management / authorised decision-maker

Handles matters requiring provider authority, approval, or a decision outside the VA's role.

These are training role orientations, not universal legal-authority rules. Always follow the provider's actual structure and procedure.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

Suppose three messages arrive:

A. “Can you update tomorrow's roster? One worker is unavailable.”

Likely area: Provider operations/admin.

B. “The family wants to know whether NDIS will fund a new type of support.”

Likely route: Support Coordinator or another authorised person, depending on Wattlebird's workflow.

C. “A participant's physiotherapy suitability needs to be assessed.”

Route: Allied Health / authorised clinical professional.

Notice the pattern:

Identify the question → identify the likely owner → check the provider workflow → route → follow up.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: A participant asks whether a particular support is funded. Who should the VA automatically answer on behalf of the NDIS?

Model answer: The VA should not make the funding determination. Route the question to the appropriate authorised role, using the provider workflow.

Question: Why can the exact owner depend on the provider?

Model answer: Different providers organize responsibilities differently. The role map is a working aid; the provider's actual procedure determines the routing.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

A role map helps you route work.

It does not give you authority to make a decision that belongs to another role.

When ownership is unclear:

ASK.

When an authorised person needs to act:

ESCALATE / HAND OFF.

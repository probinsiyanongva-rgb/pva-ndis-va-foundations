2. Why This Matters

Administrative work often arrives as short requests.

A strong VA can turn:

“Can you handle this?”

into a traceable workflow:

Owner → Classification → Action → Handoff → Follow-up → Closure

3. Learn

Open:

Sandbox → M7_Tickets

There are ten fictional provider tickets:

  • T01 — Worker is sick and cannot cover today's participant shift.
  • T02 — Two shifts appear to overlap for the same worker.
  • T03 — Family asks what NDIS will fund for a new referral.
  • T04 — Plan manager asks VA to check an invoice batch.
  • T05 — Participant requests copies of all records.
  • T06 — Participant complains about repeated late worker arrivals.
  • T07 — Worker reports a participant injury during support.
  • T08 — Service agreement is due for renewal.
  • T09 — Worker screening status is expired or unclear.
  • T10 — Participant asks whether there is enough funding for future therapy.

For each ticket, the learner fields are:

  • Your Classification
  • Your Next Action
  • What Will You Ask?
  • Who Needs the Handoff?
  • Follow-Up Trigger
  • Closure Condition

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

T01 — Worker is sick and cannot cover today's shift

The request concerns same-day coverage.

The VA can check the roster and available information, but the coverage decision belongs to the authorised operational role.

Training classification: ESCALATE

The VA's next action is to gather the relevant roster/availability information and hand the decision to the appropriate operations owner.

T03 — Family asks what NDIS will fund

This is a funding question.

The VA can acknowledge the question and route it.

Training classification: ESCALATE

The VA should not turn the question into a funding determination.

T07 — Worker reports a participant injury

This is an urgent/safeguarding-sensitive matter.

Training classification: ESCALATE

The VA should follow the provider's urgent/incident procedure and make the handoff traceable.

These examples demonstrate the reasoning pattern, not a universal rule for every NDIS provider.

5. Try It

Complete all ten tickets.

For each ticket:

  1. Read the incoming request.
  2. Identify the issue.
  3. Classify it.
  4. Identify the next action.
  5. Write the question you need answered.
  6. Identify the handoff owner.
  7. State when you will follow up.
  8. State what will tell you the task is closed.

Then review your work using the course's designated feedback/model-answer material.

The goal is reasoned routing, not memorizing a list of ten answers.

Work it here or in your own file. Saved in this browser only as you type.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

T01

Model classification: ESCALATE
Reason: Same-day coverage requires an authorised operational decision.
VA action: Check relevant roster/availability information and hand off the decision.

T02

Model classification: ESCALATE
Reason: A roster overlap needs an authorised decision about the affected shifts.
VA action: Verify the two entries and route the decision.

T03

Model classification: ESCALATE
Reason: Funding information requires an authorised response.
VA action: Acknowledge, route to the appropriate role, and track the handoff.

T07

Model classification: ESCALATE
Reason: A participant injury requires urgent responsible-person action under provider procedure.
VA action: Follow the provider's urgent/incident handoff procedure and document the handoff.

8. Work Boundary

The ticket exercise teaches routing judgment.

It does not teach the VA to:

  • approve funding;
  • make clinical decisions;
  • decide safeguarding outcomes;
  • independently approve provider terms;
  • override provider procedures.

If the ticket requires authority you do not have, the correct skill is appropriate handoff.

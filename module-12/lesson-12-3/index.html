Lesson 12.3 — Wrong Recipient, Records Requests and Authority

2. Why This Matters

Two common situations need careful handling:

  • information may have gone to the wrong recipient;
  • someone asks for records and you need to know whether they are authorized to receive them.

In both cases, the VA's role is to preserve facts, protect the information, use the approved process and keep the right owner involved.

3. Learn

A. Wrong recipient

If participant information is sent to the wrong recipient:

  1. Stop further disclosure.
  2. Preserve the known facts.
  3. Do not invent a remediation outcome.
  4. Notify the authorized privacy/operations owner through the provider's procedure.
  5. Record what happened, when, what information was involved and who received it, to the extent known.
  6. Follow the owner's instructions.
  7. Track the matter until the required handoff/closure condition is met.

Do not assume that deleting your copy of an email resolves the event.

B. Records requests

The VA should route records requests through the provider's authorized process.

The OAIC explains that an APP entity generally needs to be satisfied that an access request is made by the individual concerned or by someone authorized to act for them. The OAIC also notes that only the minimum information needed for identity verification should be sought where possible.

The VA therefore should not simply send records because someone asks.

C. Third-party authority

Consider E22 in the Wattlebird Inbox:

Liam O'Connor says he is Jack's brother and asks for Jack's plan details and next week's roster.

A family relationship stated in an email is not, by itself, enough for the VA to assume authority.

The safe administrative response is:

  • do not disclose the requested information;
  • verify authority through the provider's approved process;
  • route the request to the authorized owner;
  • record the request and follow-up;
  • wait for the approved disposition.

4. See It — Wattlebird Examples

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Should you send Jack's plan and roster to Liam because he says he is Jack's brother?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

No. The requester's authority must be verified through the provider's approved process before the information is disclosed. The VA should route the request to the authorized owner and record the follow-up.

Question: Why is deleting the wrong-recipient email not enough?

Model Answer & Reasoning

Deleting a message from one system does not by itself establish who received the information, what information was exposed, what containment steps are required, or what the provider's authorized response should be. The event needs factual recording, appropriate handoff and follow-up.

8. Work Boundary

The VA does not independently decide:

  • whether a privacy event is legally a breach;
  • whether external notification is required;
  • what records must legally be released;
  • whether a third party has sufficient legal authority;
  • what remediation the provider must take.

Those decisions belong to the authorized provider process.

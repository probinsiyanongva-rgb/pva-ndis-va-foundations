Lesson 11.1 — Read an Invoice Like an Admin

1. Progress Reminder

You have already practiced checking records, communicating findings and tracking ownership. Invoice administration brings those skills together around a financial record.

← Module 11 overview

2. Why This Matters

An invoice can look tidy while one important detail does not match its supporting evidence. A reliable administrative check compares the invoice against the relevant records before the item moves forward.

Your first responsibility is to verify the record and evidence. Approval is a separate authority step.

3. Learn

Use this nine-point check:

  1. Identify the participant — compare participant ID and name with Participants.
  2. Check the service date — confirm the date and relevant day/type context.
  3. Check the support item — compare item code and description with the fictional Price_Table.
  4. Check quantity and unit — look for missing units, unusual quantities or obvious mismatches.
  5. Check the rate/reference — use the Wattlebird fictional reference for practice. Real work uses the current official source and provider-approved workflow.
  6. Check management type — record NDIA-managed, plan-managed or self-managed when available. Management type affects the context in which a pricing or payment question is handled.
  7. Check for duplicates — compare participant, date, item, quantity and rate against related invoice records.
  8. Check service-delivery evidence — compare the invoice against M11_Service_Delivery.
  9. Record the route — identify the owner of the next decision or clarification.

For service-delivery evidence, compare the participant ID, service date, item, start/end time where available and delivered quantity. When a matching delivery record cannot be found, record no matching delivery evidence found. Keep the distinction between “evidence not found” and “service did not occur.”

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question 1: What should you inspect before treating an invoice as administratively checked?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

Check participant identity, service date, item, quantity/unit, rate or reference context, management type, duplicate risk and supporting service-delivery evidence. Then record the route and any outstanding action.

Question 2: Does an OK administrative check mean the invoice has been approved for payment?

Model Answer & Reasoning

No. An OK check means the evidence reviewed is consistent for the scope of the administrative check. Approval, submission or payment authority remains with the provider's authorized workflow.

8. Work Boundary

Your role in this exercise is administrative verification, documentation and routing. Approval, rejection, submission, dispute, pricing negotiation and funding decisions remain with the authorized provider workflow unless a documented role explicitly assigns that authority.

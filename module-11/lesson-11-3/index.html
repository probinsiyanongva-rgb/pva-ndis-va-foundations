2. Why This Matters

Different findings require different responses. A duplicate, an unclear participant, an unusual quantity and a management-type issue each have a different administrative path.

3. Learn

Pattern A — Duplicate

Wattlebird example: INV-1004

The invoice duplicates INV-1001. The delivery evidence supports one service record.

Classification: FLAG
Next action: Record both invoice IDs and ask the appropriate owner which record should be retained/disposed of.
Closure: The authorized disposition is recorded.

Pattern B — Management Type and Rate Context

Management type matters when interpreting a rate difference.

INV-1005: P02 is self-managed. The fictional charged rate is $68 and the fictional reference is $60. In this exercise, the correct classification is ASK. Ask what fictional rate was agreed and record the response.

INV-1008: P04 is plan-managed. The fictional charged rate is $205 against a fictional reference of $190, and email E06 contains an external plan-manager query. In this exercise, the correct classification is FLAG and the item routes to Olivia Grant / Plan Manager.

These figures and routing rules are fictional. Real-world price treatment must be checked against current NDIS material and the provider's approved workflow. The current NDIS pricing page says its 2026–27 maximum-price schedules apply to NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants.

Pattern C — Quantity / Service-Delivery Mismatch

INV-1011 shows 40 hours.

Open M11_Service_Delivery and find SD-011. It shows 4 delivered hours for the relevant service.

Classification: FLAG
Next action: Flag the 40-vs-4 discrepancy against SD-011 and route the correction decision. Keep the invoice record unchanged unless the authorized workflow instructs otherwise.
Closure: The authorized disposition is recorded and the required party/owner is informed.

Pattern D — Participant Identity Uncertainty

INV-1015 uses participant ID P99, which cannot be found in Participants.

Classification: ASK
Next action: Ask who the invoice is for and verify the source record.
Closure: Participant identity and invoice disposition are recorded.

Identity uncertainty is a stop point for administrative accuracy. A replacement participant should never be guessed.

Pattern E — Item Code Uncertainty

INV-1019 uses FX-XX-99, which does not exist in the fictional Price_Table.

Classification: ASK
Next action: Ask which fictional item code was intended, then route the clarified record through the appropriate workflow.
Closure: The intended item is confirmed and the invoice disposition is recorded.

Pattern F — Onboarding / Management Dependency

INV-1017 relates to P09 Grace Mitchell. The service agreement is marked Not started, while management type is To be confirmed.

Classification: FLAG
Next action: Ask Rhonda Blake whether services should have started given the onboarding dependency.
Closure: Owner disposition and required onboarding/billing action are recorded.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question 1: Why should you preserve INV-1011 at 40 hours during the initial check?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

Because 40 hours is the value recorded on the invoice. The learner has evidence that the quantity does not match SD-011, but the correction itself is an authorized decision. Preserving the source value keeps the administrative record factual while the discrepancy is reviewed.

Question 2: What should you do when P99 cannot be found in Participants?

Model Answer & Reasoning

Ask for clarification and verify the source invoice. Do not guess the participant or substitute another participant.

Question 3: Why are INV-1005 and INV-1008 handled differently in this exercise?

Model Answer & Reasoning

The fictional records have different management types and different workflow context. INV-1005 is self-managed and requires clarification of the agreed fictional rate. INV-1008 is plan-managed and has a fictional rate concern already queried by the external plan manager, so it is routed as a FLAG through the Wattlebird plan-manager workflow.

Lesson 4.1 — Melbourne, Gold Coast, and Philippine Time

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

You are building a repeatable administrative workflow. A reliable VA can tell a client not only what time something is, but also which location and date the time belongs to.

This lesson adds a third point of reference: your own Philippine Time (PHT).

← Module 4 overview

2. Why This Matters

Wattlebird has two practice locations:

  • Melbourne
  • Gold Coast

You are working from the Philippines.

That creates three clocks that can matter in one task.

A message received at a Melbourne time may arrive outside your own working window. A Gold Coast appointment can have a different PHT conversion from a Melbourne appointment on the same date.

A wrong conversion can affect: - shift coverage; - appointment reminders; - availability statements; - handovers; - response timing; - calendar entries.

3. Learn

For the Wattlebird simulation week beginning 2 November 2026:

  • Melbourne is using AEDT.
  • Gold Coast is using AEST.
  • The Philippines uses PHT.

For this simulation week:

Location Time zone UTC offset

Melbourne AEDT UTC+11 Gold Coast AEST UTC+10 Philippines PHT UTC+8

That means:

  • Melbourne → PHT: subtract 3 hours.
  • Gold Coast → PHT: subtract 2 hours.
  • Melbourne → Gold Coast: subtract 1 hour.

These are simulation-week conversions, not permanent rules. The date matters because daylight saving can change Melbourne's offset.

Victoria's daylight-saving cycle means Melbourne can move between AEST and AEDT during the year. Queensland remains on AEST. Use the date and the relevant source rather than relying on a memorized permanent difference.

Wattlebird availability

The Provider sheet gives the fictional VA working window as:

Monday–Friday, 8:30 am–12:30 pm Melbourne time.

For the simulation week, that is:

5:30 am–9:30 am PHT.

That is useful when planning your own workday.

A real reliability check

Suppose a Wattlebird email is received at 7:50 am Melbourne time.

For this simulation week:

7:50 am AEDT → 4:50 am PHT.

So the message arrived before the fictional VA's PHT working window.

That fact matters when interpreting the task timeline. It does not change the provider's stated urgency or responsibility. Follow the provider's workflow and record what happened.

4. See It

Example: E09

Open:

Inbox → E09

E09 says:

Ben asks whether his 9:00 am support is still on. His cousin in Melbourne says today is a public holiday.

Before acting, identify:

  1. Date: Tuesday 3 November 2026.
  2. Participant location: Gold Coast.
  3. Appointment location: Gold Coast.
  4. Appointment time: 9:00 am Gold Coast time.
  5. Relevant local holiday: Gold Coast, not Melbourne.
  6. PHT conversion if you need it: 9:00 am AEST → 7:00 am PHT.

The question is therefore not simply, "Is Australia on holiday?"

The useful question is:

What applies to the location of the service on that date?

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question

Why can the rule "Melbourne is always one hour ahead of Gold Coast" fail?

Model answer

Because Melbourne observes daylight saving and Queensland does not. The time difference depends on the date. For the Wattlebird simulation week beginning 2 November 2026, Melbourne is AEDT and Gold Coast is AEST, so Melbourne is one hour ahead.

Micro-proof

Write this in your exercise:

R07: 9:00 am Gold Coast (AEST) = 7:00 am PHT.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

You can convert and document times.

You can check whether a time appears consistent with the stated location and date.

You should route the matter when the conversion reveals a conflict that requires an authorized decision, such as changing a participant's support, moving a rostered shift, or deciding who covers a shift.

Do not silently change the roster because your conversion shows a problem.

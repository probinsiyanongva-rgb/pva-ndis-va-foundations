Lesson 13.2 — Reportable-Incident Awareness

1. Progress Reminder

You now know how to record an incident without adding assumptions. The next skill is recognizing when an incident needs immediate attention.

← Module 13 overview

2. Why This Matters

A VA may be the person who first sees an incident email or log entry. The VA needs to recognize urgency quickly and route the matter correctly.

The VA does not need to make the provider's legal/reportability decision to perform that administrative handoff well.

3. Learn

The NDIS Commission states that registered providers must have incident-management systems and written procedures covering how incidents are identified, recorded and reported, who incidents are reported to, who is responsible for notification, support for affected people, investigation and corrective action.

The current NDIS Commission reporting guidance also states that, for most reportable-incident categories, registered providers must submit an immediate notification within 24 hours of key personnel becoming aware, followed by additional information within 5 business days. Unauthorised restrictive practices have different reporting rules.

Course rule: These timeframes are contextual knowledge, not a memorization test. Always verify the current official source and follow the provider's procedure.

Recognize urgency without deciding reportability

Look for information such as:

  • serious injury or possible serious harm;
  • abuse or neglect concerns;
  • unlawful contact or misconduct concerns;
  • an unauthorised restrictive practice;
  • an immediate safety risk;
  • a situation where the provider's procedure explicitly requires urgent escalation.

Your action is:

RECORD FACTS → ESCALATE → FOLLOW PROVIDER PROCEDURE

The responsible provider person determines the authoritative reporting response.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

E23 reports a head injury and ambulance response.

You do not need to decide:

“This is legally a reportable incident.”

Your administrative judgment is:

“This is an urgent incident that must be escalated immediately under the Wattlebird procedure.”

That distinction protects the participant and keeps the legal decision with the authorized person.

5. Try It

Consider these fictional situations:

Scenario A: A participant is reported to have suffered a serious injury during support.

Scenario B: A participant complains that a worker repeatedly arrived late.

Scenario C: A worker reports that a restrictive practice may have been used without authorization.

For each, write:

  • urgency;
  • classification;
  • immediate administrative action;
  • owner;
  • what you will not decide;
  • follow-up trigger.

Do not assign a legal/reportable label unless the exercise specifically gives you an authorized determination.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Who owns the authoritative reportability decision?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

The provider's responsible authorized person/process. The VA recognizes urgency, records facts and escalates rather than independently determining legal/reportable status.

Question: Does knowing a reporting timeframe mean the VA should wait until that deadline before escalating?

Model Answer & Reasoning

No. The course teaches prompt escalation. A reporting deadline is not a reason to delay the provider's internal handoff.

8. Work Boundary

The VA may recognize urgency and route an incident.

The VA does not independently determine:

  • whether the event meets a legal definition;
  • whether notification to the NDIS Commission is required;
  • what category should be selected in an official report;
  • what corrective action or medical response should occur.

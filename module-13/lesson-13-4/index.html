Lesson 13.4 — Urgent and Out-of-Hours Escalation

2. Why This Matters

An urgent issue can arrive outside normal office hours or near the end of a VA shift. A responsible handoff includes the channel, owner, acknowledgement and follow-up path.

3. Learn

Wattlebird has a fictional urgent procedure:

  • Urgent channel: Wattlebird Urgent Chat
  • Primary urgent contact: Rhonda Blake — Operations Manager
  • Backup: Callum Reid — Director
  • Backup trigger: if the primary does not acknowledge within 10 minutes
  • Routine handover: email
  • Urgent handover: Wattlebird Urgent Chat
  • VA hours: Monday–Friday, 8:30 am–12:30 pm Melbourne time

These are Wattlebird house rules for practice, not universal NDIS requirements.

The urgent handoff sequence

SEND → REQUEST ACKNOWLEDGEMENT → START 10-MINUTE CLOCK → BACKUP IF NEEDED → RECORD → FOLLOW UP → CLOSE

For an urgent matter discovered outside normal office hours, follow the Wattlebird urgent procedure rather than waiting for the next day.

E23 and the timestamp gap

E23 was received at 2:15 pm Saturday.

Farah says the fall happened “this morning.”

Do not invent an event time.

Record both facts separately:

  • event time: reported only as “this morning”;
  • received time: 2:15 pm.

This is useful operational evidence because the communication channel and timing may themselves become a process-improvement issue.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

For E23:

  1. Record the factual incident information.
  2. Use Wattlebird Urgent Chat immediately.
  3. Request acknowledgement from Rhonda.
  4. Start the 10-minute backup clock when the handoff is sent.
  5. If Rhonda has not acknowledged within 10 minutes, contact Callum in Wattlebird Urgent Chat.
  6. Record the time and channel.
  7. Keep the urgent handoff open until the responsible contact acknowledges.
  8. Track the owner's procedure and closure.

Do not send the urgent handoff through routine email merely because the event arrived in email.

5. Try It

Practice A — Urgent escalation

Use E23.

Write the urgent message to Rhonda.

Include:

  • event;
  • known facts;
  • immediate action already taken;
  • urgency;
  • owner;
  • acknowledgement request;
  • backup rule;
  • follow-up.

Practice B — After-hours process gap

Add a note that:

  • the incident occurred during a morning shift;
  • the email was received at 2:15 pm Saturday;
  • the worker used a routine/shared email channel for an urgent matter.

Record this as a process observation without blaming the worker or inventing a reason.

Practice C — Audit preparation

Open Inbox and search E24 — “Audit next month.”

Rhonda asks you to tidy the incident and complaint logs and list gaps by Wednesday.

Prepare a neutral gap list.

Do not create missing evidence. Identify:

  • record/gap;
  • what is missing;
  • owner;
  • next action;
  • follow-up deadline;
  • closure condition.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: If an urgent handoff is sent at 2:20 pm and Rhonda does not acknowledge, when does the 10-minute backup clock end?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

At approximately 2:30 pm. The fictional Wattlebird rule measures the 10 minutes from the time the VA sends the urgent handoff, not from when the incident occurred or when the email was received.

Question: Should you fill in Hannah's exact event time because the email says “this morning”?

Model Answer & Reasoning

No. Record “this morning” as the source wording or otherwise preserve the uncertainty. Do not invent an exact timestamp.

8. Work Boundary

The VA does not become the incident manager, investigator, clinician, legal decision-maker or external regulator.

The VA's responsibility in this training is to:

  • recognize;
  • record;
  • route;
  • follow up;
  • preserve the administrative trail.

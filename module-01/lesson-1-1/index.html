Lesson 1.1 — What an NDIS VA Actually Supports

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A VA working with an NDIS provider is supporting the administrative side of service delivery.

Your work may sit around participants, staff, schedules, referrals, records, invoices, agreements, and follow-up.

The important skill is understanding where your administrative task fits in the provider's workflow.

← Module 1 overview

2. Why This Matters

Administrative work in an NDIS environment can affect real people and real service delivery.

A small administrative error can create a larger problem:

  • a roster entry can affect a support shift;
  • a missing document can delay a workflow;
  • an incorrect invoice detail can require clarification;
  • a message can be sent to the wrong person;
  • an unresolved item can remain open between team members.

Your role is therefore more than typing information into a spreadsheet.

You are helping keep information accurate, organized, traceable, and moving to the right person.

3. Learn

Think of the provider's work as a connected system.

Participant need → service arrangement → staff / scheduling → service delivery → documentation → billing / administration → follow-up

A VA may touch several points in that chain.

Administrative task What the VA may do
Referral intakeRecord the referral and identify missing information
Participant recordsUpdate approved administrative fields
RosteringCheck schedules and identify conflicts
Inbox managementTriage messages and route them
Invoice administrationCheck details against available evidence
Follow-upTrack unanswered questions and next actions
DocumentationKeep records organized and traceable

The VA's role is to support the workflow, not silently make decisions that belong to an authorized person.

That distinction will appear throughout this course.

Model answer

An NDIS VA supports the provider's administrative workflow by helping keep information accurate, organized, traceable, and routed to the appropriate person. The VA may perform administrative tasks across referrals, participant records, scheduling, documentation, invoices, and follow-up while staying within the role's authority.

8. Work Boundary

A VA can perform delegated administrative work.

A VA should not independently decide matters that require an authorized provider, manager, clinician, plan manager, participant, or other responsible party.

Examples:

  • changing a service decision because it “looks right”;
  • interpreting clinical information as a clinician;
  • approving a price or agreement outside the VA's authority;
  • deciding a safety or incident response;
  • disclosing participant information without an authorized basis.

When the decision belongs elsewhere, ASK or ESCALATE according to the situation and the provider's workflow.

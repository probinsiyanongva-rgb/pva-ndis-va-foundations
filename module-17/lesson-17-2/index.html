Lesson 17.2 — Trial Tasks

1. Progress Reminder

An interview tests how you communicate. A trial task gives you a chance to demonstrate how you work.

← Module 17 overview

2. Why This Matters

A trial task may be unfamiliar. The useful response is a repeatable process: understand the request, identify missing information, work carefully, document assumptions and communicate the result.

3. Learn

Use this trial-task sequence:

READ → CLARIFY → PLAN → CHECK → DO → DOCUMENT → HAND OVER

READ

Understand the requested output.

CLARIFY

Identify missing information or ambiguous instructions.

PLAN

Decide what records, sources or tools you need.

CHECK

Verify important facts before acting.

DO

Complete the task within the stated scope.

DOCUMENT

Record assumptions, findings and relevant evidence.

HAND OVER

State what you completed, what remains and any question requiring the client's decision.

4. See It

A Wattlebird-style trial task might combine:

  • a roster issue;
  • an invoice line to check;
  • an escalation message.

A strong response would show the path taken, not simply a final answer.

For example:

Finding: Roster entry appears inconsistent.
Evidence checked: Relevant roster and staff records.
Action: Documented the conflict and prepared a handoff.
Decision needed: Operations owner to confirm the approved schedule.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should you do when a trial task contains an important missing fact?

Model answer: Identify the missing information, record the assumption only when appropriate, or ask the relevant clarification before making a decision that depends on the missing fact.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Retain:

  • your completed trial task;
  • any clarification questions;
  • supporting evidence;
  • your final handover.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

A trial task does not give you authority beyond the task instructions.

If the task asks you to make a decision that belongs to the client, manager or another authorized person, identify that boundary and provide the appropriate handoff.

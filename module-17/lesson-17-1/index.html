Lesson 17.1 — Answering Interview Questions

1. Progress Reminder

Your portfolio contains evidence. An interview gives you an opportunity to explain how you think and how you approach work.

2. Why This Matters

Interview answers are stronger when they connect a real or training situation to the work you actually performed.

For this course, honesty about the difference between simulated practice and paid client experience is part of job readiness.

3. Learn

Use this structure for experience questions:

Situation → Task → Action → Result / Learning

When your example comes from Wattlebird, identify it as simulated practice.

Example

“In a fictional provider simulation, I found a duplicate invoice line. I checked the source records, documented the discrepancy, flagged it and routed it for review. The exercise helped me practice systematic invoice checking and follow-up.”

The important parts are:

  • what happened;
  • what your responsibility was;
  • what you actually did;
  • what resulted or what you learned.

Do not turn a training scenario into a claim of paid client experience.

4. See It

Question

“Tell me about a time you found an administrative error.”

A training-based answer can say:

“During a fictional provider exercise, I found an invoice issue. I checked the participant and service details, compared the relevant evidence, documented what I found and routed the issue according to the simulated provider workflow.”

That is specific without overstating the experience.

5. Try It

Prepare answers to five interview questions.

Use examples from:

  • communication;
  • scheduling;
  • invoice administration;
  • privacy/security;
  • the M15 capstone.

For every answer, mark the example as:

Actual experience or Training / simulated practice

Use only the category that is true.

Work it here or in your own file. Saved in this browser only as you type.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should you make clear when using a Wattlebird example in an interview?

Model answer: That Wattlebird was a fictional provider simulation and that the example demonstrates training/practice experience rather than paid client work.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Retain your five interview responses.

At least one response should demonstrate:

  • a source check;
  • an administrative action;
  • a boundary or escalation decision;
  • a result or learning point.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

Never claim that a fictional Wattlebird task was performed for a real participant, employer or client.

Your actual experience and your training experience can both be useful. Keep the distinction clear.

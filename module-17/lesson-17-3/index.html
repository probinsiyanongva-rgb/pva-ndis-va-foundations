Lesson 17.3 — Handling Feedback, Mistakes and Unsafe Requests

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

The first weeks of a new role will include corrections, questions and unfamiliar requests. Your response to these moments is part of professional reliability.

← Module 17 overview

2. Why This Matters

A mistake can be corrected. An unclear or unsafe request needs clarification or escalation before you proceed.

3. Learn

Use this feedback process:

LISTEN → CLARIFY → ACKNOWLEDGE → CORRECT → DOCUMENT IF NEEDED → PREVENT RECURRENCE

When a mistake is identified:

  1. understand what happened;
  2. confirm the expected result;
  3. correct it through the approved process;
  4. record the correction when required;
  5. identify how to reduce recurrence.

For unsafe or unclear requests

Use the course decision layer:

  • ASK when clarification is needed.
  • ESCALATE when authority, safety, privacy, security or another protected boundary is involved.

Examples include:

  • a request for another person's password;
  • a request to upload participant information to a public AI tool;
  • a request to make a decision outside your role.

4. See It

Request

“Just use my password for now. We need this done quickly.”

A safe response is:

“I can continue once I have the approved access path. I won't use another person's credentials. Could you confirm who should arrange or approve my access?”

This protects the workflow while keeping the communication professional.

5. Try It

Write responses to three difficult workplace requests:

  1. a correction to your work;
  2. an unclear instruction;
  3. an unsafe or out-of-role request.

For each response, identify:

  • what you understood;
  • what you need to clarify;
  • whether the correct classification is ASK or ESCALATE;
  • your next action.

Work it here or in your own file. Saved in this browser only as you type.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should your response protect when a request is unsafe or outside your role?

Model answer: Protect the workflow, information, credentials and role boundaries while keeping the communication professional and directing the request to the appropriate person or process.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Retain your three responses.

At least one should demonstrate a clear escalation or boundary decision.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

Professional communication does not require you to accept an unsafe instruction.

Follow the provider's approved process and escalate when the issue requires authority.

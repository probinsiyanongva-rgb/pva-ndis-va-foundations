Lesson 3.3 — Simple Task Triage

1. Progress Reminder

You now know how to organize files and identify versions.

The next question is:

When several tasks are waiting, what should I work on first?

← Module 3 overview

2. Why This Matters

A VA may receive several tasks at once.

Working strictly in the order they arrived can create problems when another task:

  • affects immediate safety;
  • affects an active appointment;
  • has a short decision window;
  • blocks another workflow;
  • can safely wait.

Good prioritization is a judgment skill.

3. Learn

The Wattlebird prioritization key uses five training levels:

Level Training cue
1 — ImmediateSafety, serious incident, active privacy breach, imminent service failure
2 — Time-sensitiveParticipant/service deadline or shift issue with limited time
3 — BlockingTask blocks another workflow or approval
4 — RoutineWork can be scheduled without immediate consequence
5 — Low urgency / backlogUseful work with no immediate dependency

These are Wattlebird training categories, not universal legal or employment deadlines.

The key idea is impact and dependency.

A lower-numbered category does not automatically mean “more important forever.” Context matters.

Two learners may prioritize differently when they document sound reasoning and assumptions.

5. Try It

Open Task_Board.

Using the Wattlebird prioritization categories, create a first-pass priority list for the tasks you are given in your current course work.

For each task, record:

Task Priority level Why Dependency / next action

You can type in the empty cells — entries are saved in this browser only.

Use one sentence of reasoning for each priority.

Do not simply write:

“This is urgent.”

Explain what makes it urgent.

Work it here or in your own file. Saved in this browser only as you type.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model answer

A strong prioritization record connects the level to evidence.

For example:

Time-sensitive — the task concerns a same-day shift and a coverage decision is needed before the affected shift.

Another example:

Blocking — the task is waiting on an approval that another workflow cannot proceed without.

A routine filing task with no deadline or dependency may reasonably be placed in Routine.

The goal is defensible reasoning rather than memorizing a rigid universal order.

7. Prove It

Create a Task Prioritization Record containing at least five fictional tasks.

For every task include:

  • priority level;
  • evidence / reason;
  • owner;
  • next action;
  • follow-up trigger;
  • status.

At least one task should be:

  • Immediate;
  • Time-sensitive;
  • Blocking;
  • Routine;
  • Low urgency / backlog.

8. Work Boundary

Prioritization does not mean silently changing someone else's deadline or promising an outcome.

You are deciding what deserves attention first within the information and authority you have.

If the priority depends on a missing fact:

ASK.

If a task requires an authorized decision:

ESCALATE or route it to the owner.

If an urgent safety, privacy, compliance, or active-service issue appears:

follow the provider's urgent procedure.

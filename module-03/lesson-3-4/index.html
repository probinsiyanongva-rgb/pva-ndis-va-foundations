2. Why This Matters

Training often uses realistic examples.

Real participant information is different.

A learner may become comfortable copying information between files and tools without noticing that the information has changed from fictional to real.

That distinction matters.

3. Learn

For this course:

Fictional training data

May be used in the practice environment and in your course evidence.

Real participant information

Belongs in provider-authorized systems, accounts, storage, permissions, and workflows.

This includes being careful with external tools.

A public AI tool, personal cloud drive, personal email account, or other service should not receive real participant information simply because it is convenient.

For real work, use the provider's authorized workflow and follow its instructions about approved tools and data handling.

If authorization is unclear:

ASK.

If real information may already have been exposed or sent incorrectly:

ESCALATE according to the provider's procedure.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model answer

SEC-01 — ESCALATE

Real participant information should not be pasted into a public AI tool unless the provider has explicitly authorized the tool and workflow. Stop the proposed sharing and route the question through the provider's approved data/AI process.

SEC-02 — ESCALATE

A roster containing participant names was sent to the wrong recipient. Treat it as a potential privacy issue, follow the provider's incident/privacy procedure, and keep the matter open until the responsible owner confirms the required handling.

SEC-03 — ESCALATE

Do not use another person's login. Ask the responsible system/access owner to provide the correct account or access process.

SEC-04 — ESCALATE

Do not move real participant files into personal storage merely for convenience. Use the provider-authorized storage and access workflow.

8. Work Boundary

Convenience is not authorization.

A VA should not:

  • paste real participant information into an unapproved public AI tool;
  • move real participant files into personal storage without authorization;
  • use another person's credentials;
  • send participant information through an unapproved channel;
  • assume that a tool is approved because it is free or commonly used.

Provider instructions determine the approved workflow.

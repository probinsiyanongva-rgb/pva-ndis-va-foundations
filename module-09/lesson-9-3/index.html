Lesson 9.3 — Screening and Availability Flags

1. Progress Reminder

A staff record can contain information that requires careful handling and verification. Your role is to identify what needs attention and route it correctly.

2. Why This Matters

Worker screening is not a simple colour-coded shortcut such as “green means safe to roster.” The relevant role, provider type, current status, and applicable requirements matter.

For this reason, the course teaches you to flag and verify, not to make a worker-eligibility decision yourself.

3. Learn

For registered NDIS providers, the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission says workers in risk-assessed roles and key personnel require an NDIS worker screening clearance. Registered providers are responsible for identifying and keeping records of risk-assessed roles.

A risk-assessed role is defined by the applicable requirements; it is not a blanket label for every administrative worker. The Commission's guidance describes risk-assessed roles in terms that include direct delivery of specified supports/services and more-than-incidental contact, as well as key personnel roles.

For a VA, the safe administrative workflow is:

  1. Read the staff record.
  2. Identify the status shown by the provider's system or record.
  3. Check the provider's procedure and the current official guidance when required.
  4. Record the concern without making an eligibility judgment.
  5. Route it to the authorized provider owner.
  6. Follow up according to the provider's process.

Wattlebird's fictional screening reminder

The Wattlebird workbook includes a fictional provider rule: flag screening checks expiring within 60 days. This is a Wattlebird simulation rule, not a universal NDIS requirement.

The Staff sheet may therefore show a reminder/flag that is useful for practice. Your task is to identify and route the flag.

4. See It

Suppose a Wattlebird staff record shows a screening status that is expired or unclear.

A safe note would be:

Classification: FLAG / ESCALATE
Finding: The staff record shows a screening status that requires verification.
Evidence: Staff record checked; provider procedure also needs to be considered.
Action: Route to the authorized Wattlebird owner for verification before relying on the worker record for the affected roster decision.
Boundary: I am recording the status shown; I am not independently deciding whether the worker is eligible to work.

Next-action check

Ask yourself:

  • Who makes the worker-eligibility decision? The authorized provider decision-maker under the provider's process.
  • What do I give them? The exact worker record, status shown, source checked, and the roster impact if relevant.
  • What do I avoid? A personal conclusion that the worker is or is not eligible.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Who makes the worker-eligibility decision, and what should the VA provide?

Model answer: The authorized provider decision-maker handles the eligibility decision. The VA provides accurate administrative evidence: the worker record, status shown, relevant provider procedure, affected booking, and any follow-up needed. Current official requirements should be checked when the provider's procedure requires it.

Question: Does every VA or administrative worker automatically have the same NDIS screening requirement?

Model answer: The current NDIS Commission guidance is role-based for registered providers. It identifies risk-assessed roles and key personnel rather than applying the same requirement to every worker. The provider must identify its risk-assessed roles.

8. Work Boundary

The VA does not independently decide that a person is eligible, ineligible, cleared, excluded, or suitable for a particular role based only on a spreadsheet status. Do not invent a screening rule for offshore VAs or other roles.

When the situation depends on the provider's role classification or current requirements, route it to the authorized owner and use current official guidance as directed.

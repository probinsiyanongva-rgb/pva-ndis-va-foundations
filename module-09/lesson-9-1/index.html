Lesson 9.1 — Reading a Roster

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A roster is an operational record. Your first job is to read what is actually recorded before suggesting a change.

← Module 9 overview

2. Why This Matters

Small roster errors can create practical problems: a worker may be unavailable, two bookings may overlap, a shift may have no confirmed worker, or a location/time entry may be wrong.

Good roster administration starts with evidence.

3. Learn

Read a roster row by row. Look for:

  • participant
  • worker
  • date
  • start time
  • end time
  • location or site
  • booking/status
  • notes

Then cross-check the relevant supporting record.

For example:

Roster row → Staff availability → Inbox message, if one exists → Follow-up record if unresolved

Do not treat a visual oddity as proof of a problem. Compare the records that should agree.

A simple checking sequence

  1. Open Roster.
  2. Select one booking.
  3. Note the participant, worker, date, time, location, and status.
  4. Open Staff and check the worker's availability/status information.
  5. If the booking was changed or questioned, find the related item in Inbox.
  6. Decide whether the evidence is enough to proceed, flag, ask, or escalate.

4. See It

Suppose a roster row shows a worker assigned to a shift on 4 November. Staff shows that worker as unavailable on that date.

That is evidence of a possible roster conflict.

A careful administrative note would say:

Finding: The roster shows the worker assigned on 4 November, while the staff record shows the worker unavailable that day. I have not changed the booking. This needs the authorized roster owner to decide the next step.

Notice what the note does:

  • identifies the records compared;
  • describes the finding;
  • avoids guessing why the records differ;
  • preserves the decision for the authorized owner.

5. Try It

Open Roster and inspect several rows.

For each issue you notice, record:

  • roster/booking ID if shown;
  • participant and worker;
  • date/time;
  • supporting record checked;
  • what evidence suggests an issue;
  • classification: OK / FLAG / ASK / ESCALATE.

Next-action check

Before moving on, you should be able to answer:

  1. What do I open? Roster
  2. What do I find? A booking/shift row.
  3. What do I look for? Date, time, worker, participant, location, status, and notes.
  4. What do I do with it? Compare it with the relevant staff or message record.
  5. Where do I record unresolved work? Follow_Up_Log.
  6. What does good look like? A finding supported by named records.
  7. What happens after? The appropriate owner receives a clear next action.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What evidence proves that a roster issue exists?

Model answer: The issue should be supported by the roster plus the relevant record that creates the conflict—for example, a staff availability record or a message confirming a change. A roster row by itself may show what is currently recorded, but a second record may be needed to establish why it needs attention.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Choose one roster issue from the practice data and produce a short evidence note containing:

  • record/booking ID;
  • records compared;
  • finding;
  • classification;
  • next action;
  • owner if a handoff is required.

This becomes evidence for your Module 9 roster work.

8. Work Boundary

You may check records, identify conflicts, document findings, and prepare options.

You do not independently decide which participant loses a booking, which worker receives a shift, or whether a worker is eligible to perform a role when that decision belongs to an authorized provider person.

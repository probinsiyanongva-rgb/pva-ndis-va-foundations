2. Why This Matters

A sick call or cancellation can affect a participant, worker, and provider at the same time. The administrative task is to establish the facts, identify the decision owner, prepare useful options where appropriate, and keep the follow-up visible.

3. Learn

Use this sequence:

Identify → Verify → Classify → Notify owner → Propose safe options where appropriate → Follow up → Close

Example: sick worker

  1. Confirm who is unavailable.
  2. Confirm the affected shift.
  3. Check the roster and available staff records.
  4. Identify feasible options without promising an outcome.
  5. Escalate the scheduling decision to the authorized owner.
  6. Record the handoff and follow-up trigger.
  7. Update the roster only when the authorized change is confirmed.

Example: double booking

A double booking means the roster appears to assign overlapping work. It does not tell you by itself which booking should remain.

Your job is to show the conflict clearly and send the decision to the appropriate owner.

Example: participant cancellation

Check the cancellation message, identify the affected booking, and follow the provider's procedure. Do not invent a cancellation policy or promise a billing outcome.

Example: leave conflict

If a staff record shows approved leave while a roster booking assigns that worker, treat it as a conflict requiring review.

Example: unfilled shift

If a booking has no confirmed worker, check the relevant staff/availability information and present feasible options if the exercise asks you to do so. The authorized owner decides which option is used.

4. See It

Wattlebird example: sick call

A worker reports that they cannot cover today's shift.

A useful administrative handoff might be:

Classification: ESCALATE
Finding: The assigned worker is unavailable for today's shift. I checked the roster and available staff records.
Next action: I have identified feasible coverage options for the authorized roster owner to review.
Decision needed: Confirm which option, if any, should be used.
Follow-up: I will update the roster after the decision is confirmed and check that the affected booking reflects the final arrangement.

The important distinction is prepare options vs make the decision.

5. Try It

Practice these Wattlebird issue types:

  • double booking;
  • sick call;
  • participant cancellation;
  • approved leave conflict;
  • unfilled shift.

For each one:

  1. Find the source record.
  2. Confirm the affected booking.
  3. Check the relevant staff/availability record.
  4. Classify it.
  5. State the decision needed.
  6. If appropriate, propose feasible options.
  7. State the owner.
  8. State the follow-up trigger and closure condition.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Should you change the roster as soon as you discover a double booking?

Model answer: First verify the records and escalate the scheduling decision to the authorized owner. A double booking establishes a conflict, but it does not establish which booking should be retained or changed. Once the authorized decision is confirmed, update the roster and record the resulting closure evidence.

Question: What should happen when a shift is unfilled?

Model answer: Check the booking and available staff information, identify feasible options where the task allows it, route the decision to the authorized owner, and follow up until the final arrangement is recorded.

8. Work Boundary

You may identify a scheduling conflict and propose options.

You do not independently choose which participant or worker receives an outcome when provider authorization or judgment is required. You also do not promise a participant that a particular replacement worker, time, or outcome has been approved before confirmation.

2. Why This Matters

Practice needs numbers so you can learn the checking process.

Those numbers are invented for the course.

A learner who copies a fictional Wattlebird amount into a real client task could create a serious administrative error. Keep the labels visible and keep the environments separate.

3. Learn

The Price_Table in the Wattlebird sandbox contains invented prices/limits for practice.

Use it to learn how to:

  1. identify the item;
  2. identify the fictional limit;
  3. check the participant's management type;
  4. compare the invoice line;
  5. identify an exception;
  6. decide whether to OK, FLAG, ASK, or ESCALATE;
  7. document the next action.

Management type matters

The current NDIS pricing page states that the 2026–27 maximum prices in the pricing schedules apply to NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants.

Self-managed participants have greater flexibility around pricing, so a fictional Wattlebird amount above its fictional limit should not automatically be called an error.

That is why the management-type check comes before a confident conclusion.

4. See It

Example: INV-1005

The Wattlebird practice record shows:

  • participant: P02;
  • management type: self-managed;
  • fictional invoice rate: $68;
  • fictional table limit: $60.

The correct training response is ASK, not “error.”

The reason is that the fictional comparison alone does not establish that the real-world arrangement is invalid. The next step is to ask what price was agreed in the service agreement.

This is a practice example only. The dollar amounts are fictional.

Example: INV-1008

The Wattlebird practice record shows:

  • participant: P04;
  • management type: plan-managed;
  • fictional invoice rate: $205;
  • fictional table limit: $190;
  • the external plan manager has queried the line.

The training classification is FLAG.

The VA records the issue and routes it appropriately. The VA does not correct or approve the invoice independently.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Why is a fictional price not evidence of a real NDIS price?

Model answer: Because Wattlebird's Price_Table is invented for training. It exists to practice the checking workflow and decision process. Real-world pricing must be checked against the current official NDIS sources.

Question: If a Wattlebird amount is above the fictional limit, can you automatically call it a real NDIS pricing breach?

Model answer: No. First check the management type and the relevant context. Then use the provider's procedure and route any decision that is outside the VA's authority.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

A VA may identify a discrepancy and document evidence.

A VA does not independently:

  • approve a price;
  • negotiate a participant's agreed price;
  • decide that a claim is valid or invalid;
  • submit or dispute a claim when authorization is required.

Lesson 8.2 — The Current-Source Lookup Workflow

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A good source check leaves a trail.

Someone reviewing your work should be able to see:

what you checked → where you checked → when you checked → what version applied → what you found → what happens next.

← Module 8 overview

2. Why This Matters

A link by itself is weak evidence.

A documented source check gives the next person enough information to reproduce your check.

This becomes especially important when pricing information changes between versions.

3. Learn

Use this lookup workflow:

Step 1 — Open the official source

Start with the official NDIS pricing arrangements page or another provider-approved official source.

Step 2 — Identify the document

Confirm whether you are looking at:

  • the current pricing schedule;
  • the current Support Catalogue;
  • an archived/historical document;
  • another official pricing or claiming resource.

Step 3 — Confirm the effective date

Record the date or version shown by the official source.

Step 4 — Locate the support item

Search using the support item number/code or the information supplied by the provider.

Step 5 — Check the relevant context

Look at the information that actually matters to the task, such as:

  • unit;
  • claim type;
  • claim period;
  • applicable maximum price;
  • remote/very remote context where relevant;
  • management type;
  • other conditions or provider instructions.

Step 6 — Record the source

Use the M8_Pricing_Check_Log.

Its learner fields are:

  • Check ID
  • Support Item / Code
  • Official Source
  • Date Checked
  • Version / Effective Date
  • Unit
  • Applicability / Management Type
  • Finding
  • Action / Owner

Step 7 — Compare with the provider record

Only after the source check should you compare the official information with the fictional Wattlebird record.

Step 8 — Classify

Use:

OK / FLAG / ASK / ESCALATE

Step 9 — Follow up

If something remains unresolved, record:

  • who owns the next action;
  • what is being checked;
  • when you will follow up;
  • what will count as closure.

4. See It

A strong log entry does more than say:

“Checked NDIS website.”

A stronger entry identifies the source, date, version/effective date, what was checked, and what action follows.

For example:

Finding: Support-item information was located in the current official source.
Action / Owner: Record the source and compare the fictional Wattlebird line. Escalate any approval or submission decision to the authorized reviewer.

Notice that the VA documents the finding without claiming authority to approve the final outcome.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should make you stop and ask rather than continue?

Model answer: A missing or conflicting fact that affects the check—for example, unclear management type, unclear service context, an unidentified support item, or uncertainty about which version applies.

Question: What should you do if the official source is unavailable?

Model answer: Use the provider-approved fallback if one exists. Do not guess or substitute an unverified source.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Complete at least one clean M8_Pricing_Check_Log entry and save it as part of your certification evidence.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

The lookup process is administrative work.

Approval, dispute resolution, negotiation, claim submission, and other decisions requiring provider or financial authority remain with the appropriate authorized person.

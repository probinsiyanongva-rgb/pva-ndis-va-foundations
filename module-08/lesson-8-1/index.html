Lesson 8.1 — What the Pricing Resources Do

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

Pricing work begins with a source question:

“Where should I check this?”

Start with the official NDIS pricing material rather than memory or an old copy saved somewhere else.

← Module 8 overview

2. Why This Matters

Pricing information can change. A document that was correct for one period may no longer be the current source.

A VA who records the source and effective date gives the reviewer useful evidence:

  • what was checked;
  • when it was checked;
  • which version applied;
  • what was actually found.

That makes the work easier to review and repeat.

3. Learn

For this course, two official resources are especially important:

NDIS pricing schedules

The current pricing schedules set maximum prices for NDIS supports for the applicable financial year. The NDIS states that the 2026–27 pricing schedules are effective from 24 September 2026.

The current pricing page says the maximum prices in the schedules apply to NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants.

NDIS Support Catalogue

The Support Catalogue lists available NDIS supports and provides information such as:

  • support item numbers and names;
  • claim types;
  • claim periods;
  • appropriate maximum prices;
  • remote and very remote information;
  • other claiming guidance.

The NDIS says the Support Catalogue should be used in conjunction with the pricing schedule.

What about old documents?

Older pricing documents can still be useful when researching a historical transaction, but they should not automatically be treated as the current source.

That is why effective date and version belong in your checking process.

4. See It

Imagine a provider asks:

“Can you check whether this support item is priced correctly?”

A useful first response is a workflow, not a guessed number:

Open official pricing source → identify current document → check effective date → locate the support item → inspect the relevant context → record the source.

Then compare the result with the provider's record.

5. Try It

Open the official NDIS pricing arrangements page.

Find:

  1. the current pricing schedule;
  2. its effective date;
  3. the current Support Catalogue;
  4. the statement explaining how the Support Catalogue is used.

Write these in a short note titled:

Where I Check NDIS Pricing

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Why should a pricing check record the source date or effective date?

Model answer: Because pricing information can change. Recording the source and effective date shows which information was checked and helps another person verify the work.

Question: Should you turn the module into a memorized list of real prices?

Model answer: No. The course treats real prices as lookup-only information. The transferable skill is finding and recording the current source correctly.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

7. Prove It

Create the first section of your one-page:

Where I Check and What I Do

Include:

  • official pricing source;
  • Support Catalogue;
  • effective-date check;
  • source/version recording step.

Draft or track your Prove It evidence here. Saved in this browser only — save the finished evidence in your own file with the name the lesson gives you.

8. Work Boundary

You can locate and document pricing information.

You do not independently approve a price, negotiate a participant agreement, or decide whether a claim should be submitted when that decision belongs to an authorized person.

If the question depends on a factor you cannot verify, ASK or ESCALATE.

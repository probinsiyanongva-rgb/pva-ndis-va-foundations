Lesson 8.4 — Pricing Exceptions and Boundaries

1. Progress Reminder

A pricing check is rarely just:

price A vs price B.

Context can change what needs to be checked.

← Module 8 overview

2. Why This Matters

A confident answer based on one number can be misleading.

The safer administrative habit is:

identify the missing factor → document it → route the question → follow up → close the loop.

3. Learn

Pricing work can involve factors such as:

  • management type;
  • support-item details;
  • unit;
  • claim type;
  • claim period;
  • service date;
  • remote or very remote context;
  • whether a quote is required;
  • provider procedure;
  • participant agreement;
  • historical versus current pricing information.

Historical information

If a transaction relates to an earlier period, the current document may not be the correct document for the historical check.

The relevant version/effective date must be established.

Current-source changes

Official pages can change.

The NDIS pricing page currently states that updated pricing and claiming guidance documents are to be published. That means a VA should check the current official page rather than relying on an old course note or saved document.

A useful discrepancy note

A good discrepancy note contains:

  • source;
  • finding;
  • owner;
  • next action;
  • follow-up point;
  • closure condition.

4. See It

Worked example

Finding: The line appears above the fictional Wattlebird limit.
Classification: FLAG
Action: Record the evidence and route it to the authorized reviewer.
Boundary: I will not assume the real-world claim is invalid based on the fictional comparison.
Follow-up: Check for the reviewer's response and record the final disposition.

This keeps the evidence separate from the decision.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What is the difference between identifying a pricing discrepancy and deciding the final claim outcome?

Model answer: Identifying the discrepancy is an administrative checking task. The final claim outcome may require an authorized provider, finance, plan-management, or other responsible person's decision.

Question: What should happen when management type or service context is unclear?

Model answer: Ask for the missing information or route the question to the appropriate owner. Do not fill the gap with a guess.

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

8. Work Boundary

Your role is to make the administrative evidence clearer and easier to review.

Where authority is required, route the decision to the appropriate person.

Where information is incomplete, ask.

Where an issue is urgent or requires authorized action, escalate according to the provider's procedure.

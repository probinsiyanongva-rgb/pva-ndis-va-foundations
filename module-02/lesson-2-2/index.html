Lesson 2.2 — Asking Good Questions

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

A clear message tells people what happened. A good question helps the right person make the next decision.

← Module 2 overview

2. Why This Matters

Vague questions create extra messages. A well-formed question gives the recipient the facts they need and makes the decision point visible.

3. Learn

A useful clarification request contains four parts:

  1. Reference — identify the participant, invoice, booking or task.
  2. Known fact — state what you have already checked.
  3. Uncertainty — say exactly what is missing or unclear.
  4. Decision needed — ask the authorized person what should happen next.

For example:

For Grace Mitchell’s onboarding, the agreement draft is ready and M10_Agreement identifies Rhonda Blake as the review owner. Should I route the draft to Rhonda for review?

Notice what this question does: it gives context, states the known fact, identifies the gap, and asks for the decision.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

Open M2_Incomplete_Questions.

Look at IQ-01: “Can someone cover?”

A stronger question could identify the shift, participant, date/time, and the decision needed. For example:

Joy has reported that she cannot work Marisol Reyes’s 10:00 am shift today. I checked the roster and availability and found W05 Amelia Fraser and W10 Liam Doyle as possible coverage options. Which option would you like approved? Please confirm before Marisol’s 10:00 am shift begins. I’ll update the roster after your decision.

The VA performs the evidence check that is within the role, then presents feasible options. The manager makes the coverage decision. Scenario reference: IQ-01.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question 1: Is “Is this okay?” a useful question by itself?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

No. The recipient cannot tell what “this” refers to or what decision is required. Add the record, the relevant fact, the uncertainty, and the decision needed.

Question 2: When does a question accidentally make the decision for the authorized person?

Model Answer & Reasoning

It happens when the VA presents an unsupported conclusion as though approval has already been given. The question should present the facts and ask the authorized person to decide.

7. Prove It

Complete all five rewrites in M2_Incomplete_Questions.

For each one, your question should allow the recipient to understand the situation without opening a second conversation just to discover what you meant.

8. Work Boundary

A question is not a shortcut for making a decision yourself. Ask for the information or authorization you actually need, and keep the decision with the person who owns it.

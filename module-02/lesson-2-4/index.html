Lesson 2.4 — Communicating a Mistake

Practice file: Wattlebird Sandbox Pack v2.3.4 — fictional training data. Work from a copy.Open the Wattlebird practice file

1. Progress Reminder

Reliable VAs will sometimes make mistakes. Professional communication includes knowing how to report one clearly and move the work toward closure.

← Module 2 overview

2. Why This Matters

Hiding an error can make the situation harder to fix. A prompt, factual handoff gives the responsible person a chance to contain the issue and decide what happens next.

3. Learn

Use this sequence:

  1. State the error clearly.
  2. Stop any further action on your side. Do not delete, recall, forward or resend unless the provider’s procedure instructs you to do so.
  3. Preserve the facts: what happened, what was sent or changed, to whom, and when.
  4. State the known impact.
  5. Identify what remains unknown.
  6. Notify the right person.
  7. Record and follow up until the closure condition is met.

Avoid blame, excuses and invented explanations.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

E07 is a colleague’s privacy mistake. It is useful for practicing urgent handoff, but it is not the same skill as reporting your own error.

For your own-error practice, use WD-04 in M2_Weak_Drafts: you accidentally attached an outdated fictional invoice-check file to an internal email.

The practice asset records these facts: it was sent Mon 2 Nov, 11:45 am (Melbourne) to Jamal Ortiz, and the attachment was InvoiceCheck_v1 instead of v2.

Worked model:

Hi Rhonda, I made an error in an internal email sent to Jamal Ortiz at 11:45 am. I attached InvoiceCheck_v1 instead of the current InvoiceCheck_v2. I have stopped further action and have not deleted, recalled, forwarded, or resent anything. I’m preserving the facts and escalating this for your instruction. Please confirm the approved next step. I’ll record the handoff and follow up until the closure condition is confirmed.

This model uses the facts provided in WD-04. It does not invent a remediation outcome.

Practice asset: WD-04.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: Why should you avoid writing “I deleted the email and everything is fine” unless that action and outcome are actually confirmed?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

Because the VA should record facts that are known and confirmed. An assumed remediation or closure statement can create a misleading record.

  • one notification owning your own fictional mistake;

8. Work Boundary

The VA should not hide an error, invent an explanation, decide the provider’s response to a privacy event, or declare a matter closed without evidence of closure.

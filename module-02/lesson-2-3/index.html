2. Why This Matters

Open tasks can disappear when nobody knows who owns the next step. A follow-up keeps an ordinary dependency visible. An escalation is used when authority, safety, privacy, compliance, or urgent responsibility requires another person to act.

Keep two ideas separate: classification tells you how the work should be handled; priority tells you how quickly it needs attention. A blocker does not automatically become ESCALATE. Use the Wattlebird urgent procedure when the situation meets its urgent house rule.

3. Learn

A follow-up asks about an outstanding action that is already in progress.

Example:

Hi Rhonda, following up on the roster review for Thursday’s 1:00–5:00 pm shift. Please confirm your preferred coverage option before the affected shift. If the item is still open at my 12:30 pm VA handover, I’ll record it and hand it over by email to you or the next responsible contact.

Scenario reference: E16 — use the inbox request and the current Wattlebird follow-up fields. This example demonstrates the communication pattern; it is not a replacement for the canonical operational key.

An escalation tells the responsible person that the matter requires their attention or decision.

Example:

Hi Rhonda, I’m escalating E07 because Jamal reported that Marisol Reyes’s roster was sent to Priya Nair’s email address by mistake at about 12:10 pm. This matters because participant information may have reached the wrong recipient. I have recorded the facts available in the inbox. I’m sending the urgent handoff through Wattlebird Urgent Chat and requesting your acknowledgement and confirmation of the provider’s next action. I sent the handoff at 12:20 pm. If you have not acknowledged within 10 minutes, I’ll contact Callum Reid as the fictional backup and record the time and channel. I will keep the urgent handoff open until Rhonda or Callum acknowledges it.

Wattlebird urgent procedure: Send the handoff through Wattlebird Urgent Chat, request acknowledgement, and start the 10-minute backup clock when the VA sends the handoff. If Rhonda has not acknowledged within 10 minutes, contact Callum Reid in Wattlebird Urgent Chat and record the time and channel. For E07, the earliest handoff is about 12:20 pm, so the backup point is about 12:30 pm, the same time as the fictional VA shift end. The VA must not end the shift with the urgent handoff unacknowledged or the backup contact incomplete. Record any open urgent status in Wattlebird Urgent Chat rather than the routine email handover. This is a fictional Wattlebird rule, not a universal NDIS requirement.

The key difference is ownership. A follow-up checks an existing action. An escalation transfers attention or decision-making to someone with the authority to handle the matter.

4. See It — Wattlebird Example

5. Try It

Write two messages:

Message A — Routine follow-up: Use E16 from Inbox.

How to find it: Open Inbox and search for E16, “Cover needed Thursday.”

Message B — Escalation: Use E07 and write a concise escalation to Rhonda Blake.

How to find it: Open Inbox and search for E07, “Oops, wrong email.”

Your escalation should identify:

  • what happened;
  • why it matters;
  • what you have already done safely;
  • who owns the next decision;
  • what you will track for closure.

6. Check Yourself + Model Answer

Question: What should an escalation message contain that a vague “FYI” message does not?

Write your own answer first, then compare. Saved in this browser only.

Model Answer & Reasoning

It should identify the issue, why it matters, the owner or recipient, the requested action or decision, and the follow-up/closure point. “FYI” alone leaves ownership unclear.

7. Prove It

  2. one escalation message.

For the escalation, include the Wattlebird urgent channel, acknowledgement request, 10-minute backup rule, and the time/channel you would record.

For this lesson, keep the evidence to the message itself. The follow-up fields will be introduced when the course formally models the follow-up log.

8. Work Boundary

Escalation means handing the matter to the right authority. It does not mean deciding the outcome yourself or giving clinical, legal, funding or reportability advice outside your role.
